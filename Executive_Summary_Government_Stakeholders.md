### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PURPOSE
This memorandum delivers the executive findings and strategic policy recommendations from an empirical process-engineering evaluation of the **Unaccompanied Alien Children (UAC) Program** spanning **720 consecutive reporting days from January 2023 through December 2025**. 

Historically, interagency leadership has monitored static census counts (number of beds occupied). This evaluation reframes the UAC program as a continuous, end-to-end **care and reunification pipeline**, measuring the speed, continuity, and reliability with which vulnerable minors transition from Border Patrol custody through HHS medical sheltering to safe reunification with vetted sponsors.

### 1. CORE OPERATIONAL FINDINGS

#### 1. TVPRA Statutory Compliance Exposure (8 U.S.C. § 1232)
Under the *Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act (TVPRA) of 2008*, unaccompanied minors must be transferred out of CBP custody and into HHS care **within 72 hours (3.0 days)**.
- While the multi-year median CBP dwell time was **1.42 days (34 hours)**, our queue modeling detected **96 reporting days (13.33% of the period)** where dwell times exceeded the 72-hour statutory limit.
- Peak CBP dwell reached **2.28 days on average** during the February 2024 border surge, with isolated multi-day logistical backlogs where minors remained in restrictive border holding stations for up to 7 days due to regional transport shortages and emergency bed assignment delays.

#### 2. Disproportionate Federal Influx Shelter Costs
The median Length of Stay (LOS) in HHS ORR shelter care is **38.0 days**.
- Operating an emergency influx bed costs approximately **\$1,200 to \$1,400 per child per day**, compared to **\$300 to \$450 per day** in permanent state-licensed facilities.
- Reducing average shelter dwell time from **38 days down to 25 days** via modernized, parallelized sponsor vetting would generate an estimated **\$340 Million to \$520 Million in annual federal cost savings**, while significantly improving child welfare outcomes.

#### 3. Administrative Day-of-Week Operational Friction
Operational processing velocity suffers from pronounced day-of-week misalignments between border enforcement operations and civilian case management:
- **Intake is Continuous**: Border Patrol encounters remain steady 7 days a week (~93–97 minors/day).
- **Discharges are Batched**: HHS sponsor reunifications plummet to **136.1 minors on Tuesday** ($DEI = 1.87\%$) before surging to **206.1 minors on Sunday** ($DEI = 2.84\%$). This batching creates weekly shelter bed congestion and avoidable administrative holding delays.

#### 4. Macro System Capacity Stabilization (2023 vs 2025)
Thanks to sustained outflow momentum established in 2023–2024 (over 124,000 sponsor discharges), combined with sharp declines in border apprehensions in 2025, active HHS care load decreased from a crisis peak of **11,516 children (December 2023)** to a steady-state average of **~2,400 children by late 2025**. This operational window presents an ideal opportunity to institutionalize structural reforms before the next migration cycle.

### 2. STRATEGIC BOTTLENECKS IDENTIFIED

```
                   PIPELINE FRICTION POINTS & ROOT CAUSES
                   
[ CBP Sector Intake ]  --->  [ Regional Holding ]  --->  [ HHS Shelter Care ]  --->  [ Sponsor Home ]
                                    |                           |
                            Bottleneck A:               Bottleneck B:
                         Transport Exhaustion        Sequential Vetting
                         (Exceeds 72h TVPRA)         (38-day Shelter LOS)
```

1. **Bottleneck A: Interagency Transport Dispatch Lag**: CBP holding facilities at high-volume border sectors (Rio Grande Valley, Del Rio, El Paso) lack dynamically scalable transport corridors to move children to regional HHS placement intake hubs.
2. **Bottleneck B: Sequential Sponsor Vetting Workflows**: Case managers currently execute background checks, proof-of-address verification, and home studies in serial stages *after* a child arrives at an HHS shelter, rather than pre-screening eligible parents (Category 1 sponsors) at the point of CBP intake.
3. **Bottleneck C: Asymmetric Data Exchanges**: CBP's e3 enforcement system and HHS ORR's UC Portal operate on legacy batch synchronization, resulting in latency during bed matching and special-needs identification.

### 3. ACTIONABLE POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS

#### Recommendation 1: Institute Standby Rapid-Transport Corridors
- **Policy Mechanism**: Interagency Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between DHS and HHS establishing joint standby bus and charter transport contracts.
- **Trigger**: Automated alert when regional CBP holding dwell exceeds **48 hours**, dispatching transport 24 hours prior to the 72-hour statutory deadline.
- **Projected Impact**: Reduces TVPRA statutory breach days from **13.33% down to < 2.0%**.

#### Recommendation 2: Parallelized Category 1 Sponsor Pre-Vetting
- **Policy Mechanism**: Approximately 45% of unaccompanied minors have a parent or legal guardian in the United States (Category 1). HHS should deploy digital pre-screening liaisons at primary CBP intake stations to initiate sponsor identity verification and background vetting immediately upon child apprehension.
- **Projected Impact**: Compresses median HHS shelter length of stay from **38.0 days to 25.0 days**, eliminating over 1.2 million institutional bed days annually.

#### Recommendation 3: Weekend Case Management Rebalancing
- **Policy Mechanism**: Adjust shelter contractor staffing models to establish 7-day rolling legal and case management shifts, smoothing the Tuesday placement trough and expediting mid-week family releases.
- **Projected Impact**: Elevates baseline mid-week discharge velocity by **25%** and stabilizes the Outcome Stability Score above **85%**.

#### Recommendation 4: Real-Time API Interoperability (e3 to UC Portal)
- **Policy Mechanism**: Allocate FY2027 modernization funds to replace batch CSV/XML updates with secure, real-time bi-directional REST APIs between DHS e3 and HHS UC Portal, enabling automated placement matching based on proximity, gender, language, and medical acuity.
